15 recommended papers

1. DisMUSE: An Efficient Distributed Singular-Spectrum-Based Optimization Approach with Theoretical Convergence Guarantee
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10212v1

DisMUSE develops distributed singular-spectrum optimization with Muon-style orthogonalized momentum, convergence guarantees, and a low-rank momentum-transmission variant for communication efficiency.

Why it matters to you: Direct fit for spectral optimization, Newton-Schulz/Muon methods, convergence, and communication-quality tradeoffs.

2. Finite Spectral Depth Controls Gaussian Entrance Rank and Avoidance of the First Gradient-Flow Saddle
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05022v1

Studies matrix-factorization gradient flow with finite Newton-Schulz depth and shows that depth affects early rank selection and can change which stationary point the trajectory reaches.

Why it matters to you: Directly addresses Newton-Schulz dynamics, rank selection, and optimization geometry.

3. ResQAdam: Residual-Preserving Adaptive Optimization for Memory-Efficient Quantization Aware Training
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08924v1

ResQAdam combines low-rank adaptive updates with a sign residual for QAT, reducing optimizer-state memory while preserving quality and throughput across several LLM scales and QAT settings.

Why it matters to you: Strongest direct match for memory-efficient LLM optimization, low-rank optimizer states, model quality, peak memory, and throughput.

4. From What Is Used to What Is Missing: Rethinking Dynamic Rank in LoRA
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07349v1

GEAR uses tangent-normal geometry and normal-spectrum estimates to expand LoRA rank in directions predicted to provide the largest local gain.

Why it matters to you: Direct fit for dynamic LoRA rank evolution, spectral information, and low-rank optimization geometry.

5. Spectral and Subspace Dynamics of LoRA in Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08414v1

Analyzes singular spectra and subspace motion of LoRA updates in supervised fine-tuning versus reinforcement learning, attributing differences partly to group-mean baseline subtraction.

Why it matters to you: Directly matches interests in LoRA spectral dynamics, optimization behavior, and differences between training regimes.

6. Train High, Ship Low: Efficient LoRA Synchronization for Distributed On-Policy Post-Training
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13306v1

HiLo replaces adapter transmission with compressed gradient dissemination and local optimizer replay, while Spar uses spectral truncation, adaptive rank allocation, and error feedback.

Why it matters to you: Strong fit for low-rank synchronization, communication cost, bandwidth-constrained training, and quality tradeoffs.

7. BiMuon: A Geometry-Aware Stochastic Bilevel Optimization Approach
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10188v2

BiMuon applies Muon-style orthogonalized matrix updates to stochastic bilevel optimization and analyzes spectral imbalance and iteration complexity.

Why it matters to you: Relevant to matrix-aware optimization, Muon, spectral geometry, and theoretical convergence.

8. SaMA: Morpho Adaptation via Asymmetric Expansion of Kronecker Product
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04462v1

SaMA generalizes Kronecker-based PEFT to obtain rank scaling beyond standard LoRA and analyzes how its invariance geometry affects optimizer behavior.

Why it matters to you: Directly concerns low-rank PEFT, effective rank, optimizer-friendly geometry, and parameter-efficiency tradeoffs.

9. Efficient LLM Adaptation with Forward-Only Passes
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10610v1

Presents a forward-only LLM adaptation method that uses SVD-component activation geometry to select matrices and components for rank reduction.

Why it matters to you: Relevant to SVD-based compressibility, rank reduction, adaptation memory, and speed at LLM scale.

10. Direction or Magnitude? Learning Scalars over Task-Calibrated Directions
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17784v1

SCALAR learns scalar coefficients over fixed task-calibrated directions, achieving LoRA-comparable performance with fewer trainable parameters, lower memory, and higher throughput.

Why it matters to you: Strong fit for parameter-efficient adaptation and practical memory-throughput tradeoffs.

11. The Behavioral Rate of Fine-Tuning: Compression as a Measure of Useful Information
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06598v1

Defines behavioral rate as the compressed adapter size needed to preserve fine-tuning behavior and studies how it depends on the dataset-receiver pair and correction spectra.

Why it matters to you: Directly addresses behavioral compressibility, adapter compression, singular directions, and noisy-data effects.

12. Delayed Effects of Temporary Weight Decay in Muon Pretraining
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17876v1

Studies temporary weight decay in Muon-trained Transformers across model scales and shows delayed, scale-dependent effects mediated by persistent weight-norm changes.

Why it matters to you: Direct fit for regularization, Muon optimization, norm dynamics, and training-scale tradeoffs.

13. DictLLM: Post-training Compression of Large Language Model with Dictionary Kernels
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08082v1

DictLLM performs post-training LLM compression using shared SVD dictionaries, sparse coefficients, and component-specific quantization.

Why it matters to you: Strong fit for low-rank weight compressibility, SVD structure, quantization, and memory-quality tradeoffs.

14. Theoretical Analysis of Muon on Transformers for Next-Token Prediction
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04465v1

Provides a theoretical analysis of Muon for Transformer next-token prediction, including spectral margins, convergence rates, learned weight structure, and generalization.

Why it matters to you: Directly matches spectral optimization theory for LLM training and generalization.

15. LIRA: A More Interpretable Dynamic Loss-Informed Rank Allocation for Low-Rank Adaptation
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04239v1

LIRA allocates LoRA rank dynamically using second-order loss-perturbation estimates and displacement-based rank reallocation under matched budgets.

Why it matters to you: Direct fit for interpretable dynamic rank allocation, low-rank parameterizations, and optimization-aware rank behavior.
