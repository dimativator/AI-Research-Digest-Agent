15 recommended papers

1. The Behavioral Rate of Fine-Tuning: Compression as a Measure of Useful Information
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06598v1

Defines behavioral rate as the serialized adapter size needed to retain a specified share of held-out fine-tuning gain. Reports that LoRA compressibility varies across frozen base models and that data-measured correction spectra rank rates within a base model.

Why it matters to you: Directly addresses whether low-rank compression preserves acquired behavior, rather than merely reconstructing weights.

2. Maximizing Capacity Utilization of LoRA Adapters for Mitigating the Learning-Forgetting Trade-off
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17191v1

Proposes an SVD-free Rényi-2 effective-rank regularizer for LoRA. In reported mixed-domain sweeps, it improves mean Wiki perplexity against unpenalized LoRA at tested ranks of at least 16.

Why it matters to you: Direct match for spectral regularization, effective rank, and language-model fine-tuning. Cross-method comparisons are not parameter-count matched.

3. Finite Spectral Depth Controls Gaussian Entrance Rank and Avoidance of the First Gradient-Flow Saddle
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05022v1

Analyzes matrix-factorization dynamics under finite-depth Newton-Schulz updates and shows that the number of steps can change early rank selection and bypass a gradient-flow stationary point.

Why it matters to you: Directly connects Muon-style approximation depth to rank formation during optimization.

4. Spectral and Subspace Dynamics of LoRA in Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08414v1

Tracks LoRA update spectra and subspaces in supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, reporting different spectral concentration and rotation dynamics.

Why it matters to you: Direct evidence on how training regimes affect singular-value spectra and low-rank update geometry.

5. From What Is Used to What Is Missing: Rethinking Dynamic Rank in LoRA
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07349v1

Characterizes the normal space inaccessible to standard LoRA factor gradients and uses its singular directions to motivate rank expansion.

Why it matters to you: Direct match for LoRA optimization geometry and adaptive rank selection.

6. Spectral Rank and Step-Direction Dynamics in Language-Model Post-Training
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11752v1

Distinguishes stable rank from fixed-energy rank in language-model updates and reports measurements of OLMo-2-7B spectra and post-training step directions.

Why it matters to you: Clarifies which rank statistic is informative when judging update compressibility.

7. ResQAdam: Residual-Preserving Adaptive Optimization for Memory-Efficient Quantization Aware Training
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08924v1

ResQAdam retains a BF16 momentum and uses low-rank Adam coordinates plus a residual sign update for quantization-aware training. In the reported hardware comparison, optimizer-state memory falls by 71.3%–73.2%, total peak memory by 15.3%–19.5%, and throughput by 0.6%–1.4% versus AdamW.

Why it matters to you: Strong measured quality, memory, and throughput evidence for low-rank optimizer-state compression, specifically in QAT.

8. Train High, Ship Low: Efficient LoRA Synchronization for Distributed On-Policy Post-Training
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13306v1

HiLo synchronizes distributed LoRA post-training through compressed gradients and optimizer replay; its Spar compressor combines spectral truncation, adaptive rank allocation, and error feedback.

Why it matters to you: Connects training rank to communication cost and learning quality in a distributed setting.

9. FP4Quant: Efficient 4-Bit Communication Compression for Model-Parallel LLM Training
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04323v1

FP4Quant compresses communication in model-parallel LLM training. Its abstract reports up to 1.56× end-to-end throughput improvement over BF16 on H100 with Qwen-7B, while noting a quality tradeoff.

Why it matters to you: Covers the profile’s communication, throughput, and model-quality tradeoffs beyond low-rank methods.

10. Stochastic Projections and Rank-Aware Learning Rates for Federated LoRA
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13331v2

Analyzes frozen-factor federated LoRA as a rank-dependent stochastic approximation to full fine-tuning and proposes periodically resampling the frozen factor.

Why it matters to you: Relevant to rank-dependent LoRA convergence and communication, though its federated setting may not transfer to ordinary fine-tuning.

11. LIRA: A More Interpretable Dynamic Loss-Informed Rank Allocation for Low-Rank Adaptation
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04239v1

LIRA reallocates LoRA ranks using estimated local loss perturbations from recent factor movements under a second-order approximation.

Why it matters to you: Offers a loss-based alternative to geometric rank expansion for low-rank adaptation.

12. SaMA: Morpho Adaptation via Asymmetric Expansion of Kronecker Product
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04462v1

SaMA proposes a Kronecker-product-based PEFT parameterization intended to reduce LoRA’s optimization invariances and achieve higher effective rank per parameter budget.

Why it matters to you: Directly addresses optimization geometry and rank capacity in low-rank alternatives.

13. DictLLM: Post-training Compression of Large Language Model with Dictionary Kernels
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08082v1

DictLLM compresses pretrained LLM weights using SVD-derived shared dictionaries, sparse layer coefficients, refinement, and quantization without further training.

Why it matters to you: A post-training weight-compression comparator for the goal of preserving quality under low-rank factorization; it does not test training for compressibility.

14. An Analytical Model of Compute-limited Multistage Training Pipelines
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13276v1

Studies how pretraining, noisy-label supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning interact in an analytical multistage model, with a reported OLMo-2 1B observation.

Why it matters to you: Covers the explicitly stated secondary interests in noisy supervision and compute allocation, although most analysis uses a simplified model.

15. Training Specialist LLMs on Your Phone: Forward-Only Optimization via Block-Spectral Coordinates
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04797v1

MobileSpec proposes forward-only on-device LLM specialization by training block-spectral coordinates of pretrained weights, motivated by the reported geometry of RLVR updates.

Why it matters to you: Links spectral weight parameterization to memory-constrained adaptation, though not to pretraining.
