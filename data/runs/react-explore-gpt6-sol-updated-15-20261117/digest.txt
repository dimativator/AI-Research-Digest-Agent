2026-11-03 to 2026-11-16 (inclusive, UTC)
Categories: cs.LG, cs.CL, stat.ML, cs.AI, cs.IR

15 recommended papers

1. The Behavioral Rate of Fine-Tuning: Compression as a Measure of Useful Information
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06598v1

Defines a behavior-based compression rate for LoRA adapters and finds that weight reconstruction error can misrepresent retained fine-tuning gains.

Why it matters to you: Directly addresses low-rank compressibility, singular directions, and corrupted training data.

2. Spectral and Subspace Dynamics of LoRA in Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08414v1

Finds contrasting LoRA spectral and subspace dynamics in supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, with controlled comparisons implicating group-mean baseline subtraction.

Why it matters to you: Connects LoRA optimization to effective rank and singular-subspace motion.

3. ResQAdam: Residual-Preserving Adaptive Optimization for Memory-Efficient Quantization Aware Training
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08924v1

ResQAdam preserves a QAT momentum residual while compressing optimizer state. Its reported peak-memory savings come with a small throughput decrease versus AdamW.

Why it matters to you: Directly informs memory, quality, and speed tradeoffs for low-rank optimizer-state compression.

4. Maximizing Capacity Utilization of LoRA Adapters for Mitigating the Learning-Forgetting Trade-off
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17191v1

Adds an SVD-free Rényi-2 effective-rank penalty to LoRA training and reports lower mean Wiki perplexity at tested ranks of at least 16.

Why it matters to you: Tests whether spectral regularization can change adapter rank utilization without substantially worsening the training objective.

5. From What Is Used to What Is Missing: Rethinking Dynamic Rank in LoRA
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07349v1

GEAR uses the normal spectrum to guide LoRA rank expansion and reports an improved LLaMA3-8B commonsense score with fewer trainable parameters.

Why it matters to you: Addresses the geometry and practical behavior of dynamically ranked LoRA updates.

6. Spectral Rank and Step-Direction Dynamics in Language-Model Post-Training
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11752v1

Shows that low stable rank need not mean that an LLM weight update is well represented by a small number of singular components.

Why it matters to you: Clarifies which spectral rank measures are useful when judging low-rank compressibility.

7. Delayed Effects of Temporary Weight Decay in Muon Pretraining
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17876v1

Finds delayed validation-loss benefits from temporary weight decay in Muon pretraining across four tested model scales.

Why it matters to you: Informs optimization-schedule evaluation and scale-dependent quality tradeoffs.

8. Finite Spectral Depth Controls Gaussian Entrance Rank and Avoidance of the First Gradient-Flow Saddle
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05022v1

In a quadratic two-layer model, finite Newton–Schulz depth changes early rank selection and can bypass a gradient-flow stationary point.

Why it matters to you: Provides a theoretical connection between Muon’s approximation depth and rank dynamics, not yet an LLM-training result.

9. DisMUSE: An Efficient Distributed Singular-Spectrum-Based Optimization Approach with Theoretical Convergence Guarantee
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10212v1

Studies distributed Muon-style spectral updates, finite Newton–Schulz orthogonalization, and low-rank momentum communication. Its measured compression tradeoff is on a small vision benchmark.

Why it matters to you: Addresses convergence and communication costs of matrix-valued optimization.

10. Direction or Magnitude? Learning Scalars over Task-Calibrated Directions
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17784v1

SCALAR trains scalar coefficients over fixed bases and reports lower peak memory and higher throughput than LoRA for Qwen2.5-7B fine-tuning.

Why it matters to you: Offers a measured parameter-efficient fine-tuning alternative for memory and throughput comparisons.

11. Active Learning of Approximate LLM Ranking for Energy-Efficient Retrieval of Long Queries
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16488v1

Uses active learning to approximate LLM document reranking at a controlled evaluation budget; its strongest reported result is for verbose queries.

Why it matters to you: Potentially useful for reducing LLM calls in a paper-retrieval pipeline.

12. Optimal Re-Ranking Depth
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05908v1

Studies query-specific reranking depth and reports that an LLM-assessed first-stage quality measure can reduce average depth while preserving effectiveness.

Why it matters to you: Could inform how deeply a digest agent reranks candidate papers.

13. MemCredit: From Relevance to Utility for Decision-Time Memory in Long-Horizon Web Agents
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15600v1

Proposes selecting agent memories by estimated decision utility rather than semantic relevance alone.

Why it matters to you: A possible design idea for context selection in the research-digest agent, though evaluated in other agent settings.

14. From Search to Decision: How Post-Training Shapes Information-Seeking Capabilities in Large Language Models
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14149v1

Surveys information-seeking decisions in LLM systems, including retrieval routing, query refinement, tool exploration, and evidence use.

Why it matters to you: Maps directly onto decisions required by a tool-using research-digest agent.

15. Theoretical Analysis of Muon on Transformers for Next-Token Prediction
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04465v1

Analyzes Muon training dynamics for next-token prediction in Transformers and reports convergence and spectral-margin results.

Why it matters to you: Connects matrix optimization theory to language-model training.
