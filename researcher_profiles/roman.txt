My research interests are diffusion models for text generation and the mathematical analysis of preferential attachment random graphs.

I am studying diffusion language models, particularly approaches that generate discrete text through continuous representations. Relevant topics include Gaussian diffusion on discrete data, continuous token embeddings, discrete diffusion, and self-conditioning. I want to understand the limitations of these approaches and how representations affect generation quality.

Another focus is latent diffusion for text. I am interested in jointly training an encoder, diffusion model, and decoder to obtain representations that are both easy to denoise and suitable for text reconstruction. Relevant topics include autoencoders, variational objectives, latent-space regularization, and transformer-based denoisers.

I also study the mathematical foundations of diffusion models, including DDPM, DDIM, reverse-time SDEs, probability-flow ODEs, and noise-prediction objectives. I want to understand how theoretical derivations relate to practical training and sampling procedures.

I am also interested in degree dynamics in preferential attachment random graphs, particularly the in-degree and out-degree of a fixed vertex in directed models. Relevant topics include asymptotic degree growth, almost-sure convergence of normalized degrees, moment bounds, and dependence between limiting variables. I am interested in rigorous results on covariance, independence, and limit theorems for growing random graphs.
